Why we make these

Like learning on the computer when we were kids

You got one game. It came in a box. There was a story to follow, a map to explore, a mystery to crack or a shop to run, and somewhere along the way you learned your times tables or how to type without looking. Nobody asked for a credit card every month, and nothing tried to keep you scrolling. You played until the adventure was done.

We missed that, so we're making it again for our own kids and yours:

  • The story comes first. Math, reading or code is how you open the next door, not a quiz stuck on top.
  • It grows with your kid. Our math and reading games adjust to what your kid can already do, then push a little further.
  • It ends. Every game has a real finish, and short sessions with natural places to stop.
  • It's home. Green Lake, the Ship Canal, the rain. Seattle kids get to save their own neighborhood.

For grown-ups

Questions parents ask

What's free, and what costs money?

Each game starts free, so your kid can try the first part. If they want to keep going, a grown-up buys one $4.99 unlock that opens the whole game. In our phone and tablet apps the purchase sits behind a grown-ups-only gate, so kids can't buy anything by accident.

Is there really no subscription?

Really. There's no monthly or yearly fee, no coins or gems to buy, and nothing else to unlock after the $4.99. On iPhone, iPad and Mac, Family Sharing is turned on, so one purchase covers the kids in your family.

Do the games have ads or collect data?

No ads, no accounts, no analytics and no tracking. Progress is saved on your device and doesn't get sent anywhere. Each game has its own privacy page with the details. This website has no cookies or trackers either.

What ages are they for?

Ravenna Reading is for ages 3 to 6, played with a grown-up. Most of the others are for about 5 to 9, and Maple Leaf Math, Licton Springs Language and Capitol Hill Coding stretch up to 5th grade and beyond. Each game's card above lists its ages.

Which devices do they run on?

Most of our games are for iPhone, iPad and Android. Capitol Hill Coding also runs on Windows, Linux and Mac, and Tangletown Typing is made for computers and tablets with a real keyboard. Each card above says where a game is available today.

Do I need to live in Seattle?

Not at all. The neighborhoods give every game a real, rainy, friendly place to explore, and kids anywhere can enjoy them. Seattle kids just get the bonus of recognizing the way home.

Who makes these?

We're a small family studio in Seattle. We started by making games for our own kid, and kept going. Questions, ideas or a bug to report? Email bugjugapp@gmail.com. A real person reads every message.
